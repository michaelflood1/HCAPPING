# HCA Communication Study Guide — Answers

### 1. Name a characteristic of competent communication.

- adaptability

- ability to perform skillfully

- empathy and perspective taking

- cognitive complexity

- self monitoring

### 2. What is communication competence? (2 parts)

The ability to communicate effectively and appropriately.

- Effectiveness: Achieving your goal.
- Appropriateness: Following social expectations and respecting others.

### 3. Communication is transactional. What does this mean?

Communication is a two-way process where people send and receive messages simultaneously and influence each other.

### 4. Name and explain the insights from the communication model.

- Sender and receiver: People send, receive, and interpret messages.
- Encoding and decoding: Turning thoughts into messages and interpreting them. ( meanings exist differently for different people)
- Message and channel: The information shared and how it is delivered (e.g., speech or writing).
- Feedback: The receiver's response to the message.
- Noise: Anything that interferes with communication.
- Context: The situation or environment affecting communication.

### 5. What are the 4 needs effective communication can satisfy?

- Physical: Supports health and survival.
- Identity: Helps develop our sense of self.
- Social: Builds and maintains relationships.
- Practical: Helps us accomplish tasks and achieve goals.

### 6. Define sex and gender (1 line each).

- Sex: Biological characteristics such as reproductive organs, hormones, and chromosomes.
- Gender: Socially and culturally shaped roles, behaviours, identities, and expressions.

### 7. What is culture? (1 line)

Culture is the shared beliefs, values, customs, traditions, and behaviours of a group.

### 8. Difference between cultural safety and cultural humility.

- Cultural safety: Creating an environment where clients feel respected, safe, and free from discrimination; the client determines whether care is culturally safe.
- Cultural humility: Continuously reflecting on your own biases, recognizing power differences, and learning from others.

### 9. What is low-context vs. high-context communication?

- Low-context: Messages are direct, explicit, and rely mainly on words.
- High-context: Messages rely more on relationships, shared understanding, body language, and context.

### 10. What is cultural competence?

The ability to understand and work respectfully and effectively with people from different cultural backgrounds.

### 11. Give 3 ways to increase intercultural competence.

- Learn about other cultures without stereotyping.
- Reflect on your own biases and assumptions.
- Ask respectful questions and listen with an open mind.
- Build relationships with people from different backgrounds.
- Learn about cultural differences in communication.
- Accept feedback and continue learning.

### 12. How can you show a client you are actively listening?

- Maintain appropriate eye contact.
- Face the client and use open body language.
- Avoid interrupting or distracting yourself.
- Nod and use brief verbal responses.
- Paraphrase and clarify what they say.

### 13. What are the two types of questioning responses? Give examples.

- Open-ended questions: Encourage detailed answers. Example: “How are you feeling today?”
- Closed-ended questions: Require a short or specific answer. Example: “Are you feeling pain?”

### 14. What is caring, and how do you show care?

Caring means showing concern for another person's well-being. Show it by listening, respecting their choices, being patient, offering help, and treating them with dignity.

### 15. Client says, “I am so sick and tired of my body not working the way it used to. I hate having to ask for help all the time.” Give a paraphrasing response.

“It sounds like you're frustrated by the changes in your body and having to depend on others for help.”

### 16. Define self-concept and describe its three characteristics.

Self-concept: How a person sees and understands themselves.

- subjective

- resists change

- flexible

### 17. Give two characteristics of someone with positive self-esteem.

- Feels confident and values themselves.
- Accepts their strengths and weaknesses.

### 18. What are two theories explaining how we develop self-concept?

- Looking-glass self (Charles Cooley): We develop our self-image partly by imagining how others see and judge us.
- Social comparison theory (Leon Festinger): We evaluate ourselves by comparing our abilities, achievements, or traits with others.

### 19. Give three examples of showing warmth as an HCA.

- Smile and greet clients in a friendly manner.
- Use a gentle, respectful tone of voice.
- Show genuine interest, listen attentively, and offer reassurance.

### 20. What guidelines should you follow when using humour at work?

- Ensure it is appropriate to the situation.
- Never use humour that is discriminatory, offensive, or humiliating.
- Consider the client's culture, feelings, and sense of humour.
- Never joke about a client's condition or vulnerability.
- Stop if the client appears uncomfortable.
- Use humour to build rapport, not to avoid serious concerns.

### 21. Define the different types of communication noise and give examples.

- Physical noise: Environmental distractions, e.g., a loud TV.
- Physiological noise: Physical conditions affecting communication, e.g., hearing loss or pain.
- Psychological noise: Thoughts or emotions interfering with listening, e.g., anxiety or anger.
- Semantic noise: Confusion over the meaning of words, e.g., medical jargon a client does not understand.

### 22. What is active listening? Give at least two points.

Active listening means fully concentrating on, understanding, and responding to a speaker.

- Give the speaker your full attention without interrupting.
- Paraphrase or summarize to confirm understanding.
- Ask clarifying questions.
- Notice non-verbal cues.

### 23. What can affect how someone receives a message? Why does the same message differ from person to person?

- Culture, past experiences, beliefs, values, emotions, expectations, language, and personal circumstances.
- People interpret messages differently because they have different backgrounds, experiences, and perspectives.

### 24. Explain two characteristics of non-verbal communication.

- Continuous: Body language and facial expressions communicate even when we are not speaking.
- Ambiguous: The same gesture or expression can have different meanings depending on the person and context.

### 25. How can non-verbal communication influence how your message is received?

- Facial expressions: Smiling can show friendliness; frowning may suggest concern.
- Tone of voice: A calm tone can reassure; a harsh tone can sound disrespectful.
- Distance: Appropriate personal space shows respect; standing too close may feel threatening.

Your non-verbal cues should match your words to communicate trust and care.

### 26. What is the empathy formula? Give examples.

Empathy formula: Acknowledge the person's feelings + identify the reason for those feelings.

Examples:

- “You seem worried because you don't know what will happen next.”
- “You sound frustrated because you need more help than before.”
- “You seem sad because you miss being able to do things independently.”

### 27. What are the three dimensions of empathy?

- Cognitive empathy: Understanding another person's perspective and feelings.
- Emotional empathy: Emotionally relating to or sharing another person's feelings.
- Compassionate empathy: Understanding and relating to their feelings while being motivated to help.

### 28. Differentiate between beliefs, values, and attitudes. Give examples and explain how they influence self-concept.

- Beliefs: Things we think are true. Example: “I believe I can learn new skills.”
- Values: Things we consider important. Example: “Honesty matters to me.”
- Attitudes: Positive or negative feelings or opinions about something. Example: “I enjoy helping people.”

Influence on self-concept: These shape how we view ourselves, judge our actions, and decide what kind of person we are.

### 29\. What is self-disclosure, and what should an HCA consider before using it with a client?

Self-disclosure is sharing personal information or experiences about yourself with another person.

Before using it, an HCA should consider:

- Purpose: Will it benefit the client?
- Boundaries: Is it professional and appropriate?
- Relevance: Does it relate to the client's situation?
- Client's needs: Will it support them or shift attention to the HCA?
- Privacy: Avoid sharing unnecessary personal or confidential information.
