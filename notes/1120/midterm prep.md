## 1. Dimensions & Determinants of Health; Holism, Health Promotion & Disease Prevention

**1) What does 'holism' mean?**
Looking at the whole of something rather than just its parts. The whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

**2) Holism in health:**
A person is a whole being made up of interconnected dimensions (physical, emotional, social, spiritual, cognitive). A problem in one area affects all the others, so care must address the whole person, not just the illness or symptom.

**3) Matching:**
- a. Physical health → **achieved when the body is strong fit and free of disease**
- b. Emotional health → **achieved when people feel good about themselves**
- c. Social health → **achieved through stable and satisfying relationships**
- d. Spiritual health → **achieved through belief in a purpose greater than the self**
- e. Cognitive health → **achieved through an active creative mind**

**4) "Health is a continuum":**
Health isn't just "healthy" or "sick." It is a range from poor health/premature death at one end to optimum wellness at the other. People move back and forth along it throughout life depending on their choices, circumstances and conditions.

**5) Optimum wellness:**
The highest level of health a person can reach: all dimensions (physical, emotional, social, spiritual, cognitive) are balanced and working well, and the person actively makes choices to maintain and improve well-being (check text).

**6) Personal factors that influence health (Mosby's):**
Heredity/genetics, age, sex/gender, lifestyle habits (nutrition, exercise, sleep, stress management, tobacco/alcohol/drug use), personal values and beliefs, education, income, and environment (check text).

**7) Personal factor that influenced my health (sample):**
Regular exercise has improved my mood, energy and sleep. Stress from juggling work and school has sometimes hurt my sleep and eating habits.

**8) The 12 Determinants of Health (Public Health Agency of Canada):**
1. Income and social status
2. Social support networks
3. Education and literacy
4. Employment/working conditions
5. Social environments
6. Physical environments
7. Personal health practices and coping skills
8. Healthy child development
9. Biology and genetic endowment
10. Health services
11. Gender
12. Culture

**9) A determinant that affected my health (sample):**
Social support networks. Having supportive family and friends helps me cope with stress and stay motivated. (Or: Education, which gives me the knowledge to make healthier choices.)

**10) Health promotion:**
The process of enabling people to increase control over, and improve, their health through education, supportive policies and environments. Example: a smoking-cessation program, a school immunization clinic, or a community walking group.

## 2. Exercise and Weight Management

**1) Why exercise?**
It strengthens the heart and lungs, builds muscle and bone, controls weight, lowers risk of heart disease, diabetes, stroke, some cancers and osteoporosis, improves mood, reduces stress, helps sleep, and maintains independence as we age.

**2) Characteristics of fitness:**
Cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, and healthy body composition.

**3) Cardiovascular endurance:**
The ability of the heart, lungs and blood vessels to supply oxygen to the body during sustained physical activity over time.

**4) Overload principle:**
To improve fitness, you must challenge the body a little more than it is used to (more intensity, time or frequency). The body adapts and gets stronger, and you keep progressing gradually.

**5) Why weight is not a good measure of body fat:**
Scales don't separate fat from muscle, bone and water. Muscle is denser than fat, so a fit, muscular person can weigh more than a less fit person with more body fat. Body composition (percentage of fat), waist circumference or BMI plus waist measures are more useful.

**6) Risks of being overweight:**
Heart disease, high blood pressure, stroke, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol, some cancers, sleep apnea, osteoarthritis/joint problems, gallbladder disease, depression and low self-esteem.

**7) Risks of being underweight:**
Malnutrition, weak immune system, anemia, fatigue, osteoporosis/fractures, hormonal and fertility problems, slow wound healing, feeling cold, and possible underlying illness or eating disorder.

**8) Fat storage at highest risk:**
The **abdomen/waist** (central or "apple-shaped" obesity).

**9) Why most diets don't work:**
They are restrictive and temporary. People lose weight (often water and muscle), the metabolism slows, hunger and cravings increase, and when the person returns to old habits the weight comes back. Lasting change requires permanent lifestyle changes, not a short-term fix.

**10) Five strategies for healthy weight and body fat:**
1. Eat a balanced diet with appropriate portion sizes (Canada's Food Guide).
2. Be physically active most days (at least 150 min/week of moderate activity plus strength training).
3. Limit processed foods, sugary drinks and high-fat/high-sugar snacks.
4. Eat mindfully and don't skip meals.
5. Get enough sleep, manage stress, and set realistic goals while tracking progress.

## 3. Nutrition

**1) Terms:**
- **Nutrition:** the processes by which the body takes in and uses food for growth, repair and energy.
- **Ingestion:** taking food into the body through the mouth (eating).
- **Digestion:** the mechanical and chemical breakdown of food into forms the body can absorb.

**2) Why nutrition is important:**
It provides energy, supports growth and repair, regulates body processes, supports the immune system, and prevents illness (e.g. heart disease, diabetes, osteoporosis).

**3) Nutrient:**
A substance in food needed by the body for energy, growth, repair and regulation. The 6 nutrients: **carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water**.

**4) Calories per gram:**
Protein = 4, Carbohydrate = 4, Fat = 9.
Fat has more than twice the calories, so high-fat foods are very calorie-dense. Small portions add up quickly, so I need to watch fat portions to manage weight (while still eating healthy fats).

**5) Complete vs incomplete protein:**
- **Complete:** contains all 9 essential amino acids (meat, fish, poultry, eggs, dairy, soy, quinoa).
- **Incomplete:** lacks one or more essential amino acids (most plant foods like beans, grains, nuts). Combining plant proteins during the day can make up for this.

**6) Four types of carbs (check text):**
Sugars (simple carbs), starches (complex carbs), fibre, and glycogen (stored carbohydrate in liver/muscle).

**7) Change in carb intake over 100 years:**
We now eat far more refined and processed carbohydrates and added sugars (white flour, sweetened drinks, snack foods) and less fibre and whole grains, fruits and vegetables.

**8) Three main types of fat:**
Saturated fat, unsaturated fat (mono- and polyunsaturated), and trans fat. (Some texts list cholesterol as well; check text.)

**9) Vitamins:**
- Vit D → absorption of calcium
- Vit K → blood clotting
- Vit A → vision
- Vit B3 (Niacin) → nervous system function

**10) Minerals:**
- Sodium → fluid balance
- Iodine → thyroid gland function
- Calcium → teeth & bones, blood clotting, nerve & muscle function
- Iron → allows red blood cells to carry oxygen
- Potassium → nerve, muscle/heart function
- Phosphorus → bones & teeth; use of proteins, carbs & fats; nerve & muscle function
- Zinc → growth, healing, immune system

**11) Why a range of servings?**
Needs vary with age, sex, body size, activity level, and life stage (e.g. pregnancy, growth).

**12) Item listed first on a food label:**
It is the ingredient present in the **greatest amount (by weight)** in the product.

**13) Percentage of calories from fat:** about **20-35%**.

**14) Percentage of calories from carbs:** about **45-65%**.

**15) Nutrition in older adults:**
- **Emotional:** loneliness, depression, grief, boredom, or anxiety can reduce appetite or cause overeating; loss of a spouse who cooked.
- **Social:** eating alone, limited income, transportation problems getting groceries, loss of social meals, cultural food preferences not met.
- **Physical:** decreased sense of taste/smell, dental problems or dentures, swallowing difficulty, chronic illness, medication side effects, reduced mobility or dexterity for cooking, slower metabolism, decreased thirst.

**16) Factors affecting appetite/eating habits:**
Hunger/satiety signals, emotions and stress, culture and religion, family and social customs, food availability and cost, advertising, personal preferences, health and medications, time and convenience, and the sight, smell and presentation of food.

## 4. Change Process and SMART Goals

**1) What is change?**
Making something different; a process of moving from one way of being or doing things to another. It can be planned or unplanned, and can bring stress and growth.

**2) 4 C's for coping with change (check text/class notes):**
clarity commitment compassion consistency check notes on this one

**3) When am I ready for change?**
When I recognize the need, believe the benefits outweigh the costs, feel confident I can succeed, and am willing to take action and plan. Readiness shows in the stages below.

**4) Five stages of the Transtheoretical Model:**
1. Precontemplation (not thinking about changing)
2. Contemplation (thinking about it, weighing pros and cons)
3. Preparation (planning, getting ready)
4. Action (actively making the change)
5. Maintenance (sustaining the change, preventing relapse)
(Some versions add Termination.)

**5) SMART goals:**
Goals that are **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**ealistic/Relevant, and **T**ime-bound.

**6) Three SMART goal examples:**
1. "I will walk briskly for 30 minutes, 4 days a week (Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat), for the next 8 weeks."
2. "I will eat at least 5 servings of vegetables and fruit daily for the next 6 weeks, tracking in a journal."
3. "I will go to bed by 10:30 p.m. on weeknights and get 7-8 hours of sleep for the next month."

**7) Wheel of Life activity:**
Draw a circle divided into areas (e.g. health, career, finances, family, friends, relationships, personal growth, fun/recreation, environment, spirituality). Rate your satisfaction in each from 1 (centre) to 10 (edge), shade it in, and see where the wheel is "bumpy." Choose areas to improve for your change project.

## 5. Workplace Settings

**1) Definitions:**
- **a. Acute care:** short-term treatment for severe illness, injury or surgery, usually in a hospital.
- **b. Sub-acute/convalescent care:** care for people recovering after acute illness/surgery who need more time and support before going home.
- **c. Long-term care:** 24-hour residential care for people who can no longer live independently and need ongoing support and supervision.
- **d. Respite care:** temporary care that gives family caregivers a break (hours, days or weeks).
- **e. Rehabilitation services:** therapy to help people regain or maximize function after injury, illness or surgery (physio, OT, speech therapy).
- **f. Palliative care/hospice:** comfort-focused care for people with life-limiting illness, supporting dignity, symptom control and family.
- **g. Mental health services:** assessment, treatment and support for people with mental illness or substance use issues, in hospitals or community.

**2) Table:**

| Setting | Brief description | Responsibilities of HCSW | Issues/Challenges |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Home Care** | Support for clients living in their own homes | Personal care, light housekeeping, meal prep, medication reminders, mobility support, companionship, reporting changes | Working alone, isolation, safety of home environment, limited supervision, travel between clients, unclear boundaries |
| **Community Day Program** | Daytime programs for seniors/adults with disabilities or dementia | Assist with activities, personal care, meals, supervision, social engagement | Behaviours, varied client needs, staffing levels, transportation |
| **Retirement Homes** | Private housing for independent seniors with optional services | Assist with limited ADLs, meals, safety checks, social activities | Clients' changing needs, limited staff, scope of practice, family expectations |
| **Assisted Living** | Housing plus personalized support services (meals, personal care, medication help) | Personal care, medication assistance within scope, monitoring, emotional support | Balancing independence with safety, dementia progression, documentation |
| **Residential/Long-Term Care Facility** | 24-hour care for people with complex needs | Full personal care, mobility/transfers, feeding, toileting, behaviour support, teamwork with nurses | Heavy workload, staffing shortages, physical strain, emotional stress, end-of-life care, abuse risk |
| **Group Homes** | Home-like setting for people with developmental/physical disabilities or mental health needs | Daily living support, skill building, community outings, behaviour support | Challenging behaviours, shift work, building trust, limited resources |
| **Working directly for client/family** | Private hire arrangement | Duties set with the family: care, housekeeping, meals, companionship | No agency backup, unclear scope/contract, pay and hours disputes, boundary issues, liability |

## 6. Canadian Health Care System

**1) Impact of the Depression on clients:**
Many are very thrifty, avoid waste, save and reuse items, distrust banks or debt, may hoard, and are reluctant to accept help, spend money, or throw out food. They value hard work and independence, so accepting care can be hard.

**2) Canada Health Act (1984) and the 5 principles:**
Federal law setting conditions for provinces to receive federal health funding.
- **Public administration:** run by a non-profit public authority.
- **Comprehensiveness:** covers all medically necessary hospital and physician services.
- **Universality:** all insured residents are covered on equal terms.
- **Portability:** coverage continues when moving or travelling within Canada (and abroad, with limits).
- **Accessibility:** reasonable access to care without financial or other barriers.

**3) Federal government's role:**
Sets and enforces national standards (Canada Health Act), provides funding to provinces/territories, delivers care to some groups (First Nations and Inuit, military, veterans, federal inmates), and handles health protection, research and public health.

**4) Provincial government's role:**
Plans, funds, administers and delivers health services; manages hospitals, insures physician services, licenses professionals, and runs programs like home care and long-term care.

**5) Levels of care:**
- **a. Primary:** first point of contact and ongoing care (family doctor, clinics, nurse practitioners, health promotion).
- **b. Secondary:** specialist or hospital care by referral (surgeons, specialists).
- **c. Tertiary:** highly specialized care in major centres (cancer care, neurosurgery, transplants).

**6) Four challenges (check text):**
Aging population, rising costs, shortage of health professionals/wait times, and new technology/treatments and unequal access (rural/remote). Use your text's wording.

**7) Health promotion:**
Helping people increase control over and improve their health through education, healthy public policy and supportive environments.

**8) Home care and its goal:**
Health and support services delivered in a person's home. The goal is to help people stay in their homes as independent as possible for as long as possible, delay or avoid facility placement, and support recovery or end-of-life care.

**9) Why home care differs by province:**
Health care is a provincial/territorial responsibility, and home care isn't fully covered under the Canada Health Act. Each province sets its own policies, funding, eligibility and fees.

**10) Scenario You are traveling in Germany. One day a German lady asks you to explain what home support workers do. What will you say? (explaining to a German lady):**
"A home support worker goes into people's homes to help them with everyday needs, such as bathing, dressing, eating, moving safely, light housekeeping and meals, so older adults or people with illness or disability can stay at home safely and with dignity. We work with nurses and other health professionals and also provide companionship."

## 7. Roles, Qualities & Restrictive Activities of an HCA

**1) Goal of support work:**
To help clients maintain or improve independence, dignity, comfort and quality of life while meeting their physical, emotional and social needs.

**2) Titles:**
BC textbook title: **Health Care Assistant (HCA)**. Others: home support worker, community health worker, resident care attendant, care aide, personal support worker (Ontario), health care support worker. Variety exists because of different employers, settings, funding models, regions and historical roles/training.

**3) Titles for people you care for:**
Client, patient, resident, consumer, person in care, service user.

**4) Nine groups of people (Wilk et al., 2022) (check text):**
Typically: older adults; people with dementia; people with chronic illness; people with physical disabilities; people with developmental disabilities; people with mental health challenges; people with substance use issues; people receiving palliative/end-of-life care; people recovering from acute illness/surgery (and sometimes children/youth). 

**5) Health care team members and roles:**
- **Registered Nurse (RN):** assesses, plans and provides care, delegates tasks.
- **Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN):** provides nursing care under defined scope.
- **Physician/Nurse Practitioner:** diagnose, treat, prescribe.
- **Physiotherapist (PT):** mobility, strength, exercise.
- **Occupational Therapist (OT):** daily living skills, adaptive equipment.
- **Speech-Language Pathologist/Dietitian:** communication and swallowing; nutrition.
- **Social worker/Case manager:** counselling, resources, care planning.
- **Pharmacist:** medications.
- **Recreation therapist:** activities and social engagement.
- **HCA:** direct daily care, observation and reporting.
- **Client/family:** central team members.

**6) Six categories of responsibilities (check text):**
1. Personal care (bathing, grooming, dressing)
2. Mobility/safety (transfers, positioning)
3. Nutrition support (meal prep, feeding)
4. Household management (laundry, light cleaning)
5. Emotional/social support (companionship, communication)
6. Observing, reporting and documenting (changes in condition, charting)

**7) Elements of professionalism:**
Reliability and punctuality, appropriate appearance and hygiene, positive attitude, respect, confidentiality, honesty, accountability, respectful communication, teamwork, staying within scope, and ongoing learning.

**8) Other qualities:**
Compassion, patience, empathy, trustworthiness, flexibility, good communication, good judgment, physical stamina, cultural sensitivity, and willingness to ask for help.

**9) Scope of practice:**
The tasks and responsibilities an HCA is educated, authorized and competent to perform. It is determined by provincial legislation/regulations, the employer's policies, education/training, and the care plan, with delegation by regulated professionals.

**10) Terms:**
- **Tasks:** specific pieces of work or care activities.
- **Restricted activities:** higher-risk procedures that only regulated professionals may perform unless properly delegated/authorized (e.g. injections, wound care, inserting tubes).
- **Delegation process:** a regulated health professional (e.g. RN) assigns a task to an HCA after teaching, assessing competence, and providing supervision.
- **Who delegates:** regulated health professionals such as RNs, LPNs, OTs and PTs (within their scope).

**11) Regulated vs non-regulated:**
- **Regulated:** governed by a college and legislation with licensing, standards and discipline (nurses, doctors, physios).
- **Non-regulated:** not governed by a college (HCAs in BC are on a registry but not self-regulated).
Important because it determines accountability, scope, who can delegate, and who is responsible for errors.

**12) Policies and procedures:**
- **Policies:** employer rules and guidelines for decisions and behaviour.
- **Procedures:** step-by-step instructions for how to do a task.
Common examples: infection control/hand hygiene, confidentiality, fire and emergency, safe lifting/transfers, abuse reporting, incident reporting, medication assistance, and WHMIS.

## 8. Legal Issues

**1) Table:**

| Legal Term | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Criminal Law** | Laws about offences against society/the state, enforced by government | Theft from a client, physical assault |
| **Civil Law** | Laws about disputes between individuals/organizations | Client sues for injury caused by careless care |
| **Tort** | A wrongful act (not a breach of contract) causing harm, leading to a civil lawsuit | Harm resulting from an unsafe act |
| **Intentional Tort** | A deliberate wrongful act | Hitting or threatening a client |
| **Unintentional Tort** | Harm caused unintentionally (careless act) | Dropping a client during a transfer |
| **Negligence** | Failing to give the care a reasonable person would, causing harm | Leaving a bed rail down and the client falls |
| **Assault** | Threatening or attempting to touch someone without consent, creating fear | Threatening to hit a client who refuses care |
| **Battery** | Touching a person without consent | Giving a bath after the client refused |
| **False Imprisonment** | Unlawfully restricting a person's freedom of movement | Locking a client in a room or using restraints without authorization |
| **Invasion of Privacy** | Violating a person's right to privacy/confidentiality | Sharing client information with friends; exposing a client during care |
| **Defamation of Character** | False statements that harm someone's reputation | Falsely telling others a client is a thief |
| **Libel** | Defamation in writing | Writing false accusations in a chart |
| **Slander** | Defamation spoken aloud | Falsely telling coworkers a resident is abusive |

**2) Scope of practice:**
The tasks and duties an HCA is legally allowed, trained and authorized to perform.

**3) Malpractice:**
Professional negligence: harm to a client caused by a professional's failure to meet the standards of their profession.

**4) Sources to understand scope of practice:**
Provincial HCA program standards/competencies, employer job description, policies and procedures, care plans, supervisors/delegating nurses, the Registry, and relevant legislation.

**5) Avoiding personal liability:**
Work within scope, follow care plans and policies, ask when unsure, refuse unsafe tasks, document accurately, report errors promptly, maintain competence, and carry liability insurance if self-employed.

**6) Avoiding legal liability:**
Obtain consent, protect confidentiality and privacy, use safe techniques, document and report accurately, respect client rights, report abuse, and never perform restricted activities without delegation.

## 9. Protection and Abuse

**1) Right to life, liberty and security:**
The **Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms**.

**2) Legislation against age discrimination:**
**Human rights legislation** (e.g. BC Human Rights Code, Canadian Human Rights Act).

**3) BC legislation for long-term care/assisted living:**
The **Community Care and Assisted Living Act** (with its regulations, e.g. the Residential Care Regulation).

**4) Four common client rights (check text):**
Dignity and respect; privacy and confidentiality; choice/autonomy/participation in decisions; safety and freedom from abuse. (Your text may use similar wording such as consent or access to information.)

### Abuse

**1) What is abuse?**
Intentional or unintentional harmful acts or neglect that cause harm, distress or loss of rights, usually by someone in a position of trust or power.

**2) Five types (Sorrentino) with examples:**
1. **Physical:** hitting, slapping, rough handling, inappropriate restraint
2. **Psychological/emotional/verbal:** threats, humiliation, insults, isolation
3. **Sexual:** unwanted touching, sexual comments, exposure
4. **Financial/material exploitation:** stealing money, misusing bank accounts
5. **Neglect:** failing to provide food, hygiene, medication or safety

**3) Cycle of abuse:**
A repeating pattern: (1) tension building, (2) abusive incident, (3) reconciliation/"honeymoon" (apology, promises), (4) calm, then tension builds again.

**4) Contributing factors:**
Stress, caregiver burnout, substance use, history of abuse, power and control issues, lack of support, financial strain, dependence, cognitive impairment, social isolation, and lack of training.

**5) Higher-risk groups:**
Older adults, people with disabilities, people with dementia or cognitive impairment, women, children, isolated or dependent people, and those with language barriers or limited resources.

**6) How worker stress contributes:**
Overwork, understaffing, burnout and frustration can lead to impatience, rough handling, neglect, or emotional outbursts toward clients.

**7) If you suspect abuse:**
Ensure safety, observe and document facts objectively, and report immediately to your supervisor/nurse (or per agency policy/legal reporting). Don't confront the suspected abuser or investigate yourself. In BC, you can also report to the health authority, adult protection services (Designated Agency) or police if in danger.

**8) If I am being abused as a HCSW:**
Tell the person to stop (if safe), remove myself from danger, report to my supervisor, document the incident, and use agency procedures, the union and/or police. In **complex care/assisted living** there is a supervisor and team to report to and help; in **home support** I'm alone, so I may need to leave the home, call my agency immediately, and call 911 if unsafe.

**9) Reasons clients act in a sexually abusive manner:**
Dementia or cognitive impairment, confusion (mistaking worker for spouse), disinhibition from brain injury or medication, loneliness or need for touch/intimacy, mental illness, misunderstanding care touch, learned behaviour, or deliberate intent to control.

**10) Assertive responses:**
Stay calm, make eye contact and use a firm, clear voice: "Please stop. I'm here to help you with your care. That behaviour is not acceptable." Step back, set limits, redirect, and report and document. Do not laugh or ignore.

**11) Legal responsibility regarding abuse:**
You must report suspected abuse; it is both an ethical and legal duty (check text). Failing to report may result in disciplinary or legal consequences. Report in good faith, and you are protected.

**12) If a client tells you they are being abused:**
Stay calm, listen without judgment, believe them, reassure them it's not their fault, don't promise secrecy, ensure safety, document their words, and report right away to your supervisor/nurse.

## 10. Ethics

**1) Ethics and Code of Ethics:**
- **Ethics:** principles of right and wrong that guide behaviour/decisions in a profession or society.
- **Code of Ethics:** a written set of rules/standards for professional conduct.

**2) Morals:**
Personal beliefs about right and wrong, developed from family, culture, religion and experience.

**3) Difference:**
Morals are personal and internal; ethics are shared, external professional standards or rules.

**4) How culture influences:**
Culture shapes our values, beliefs and ideas of right and wrong (e.g. views on family roles, illness, death, autonomy), which in turn shape morals and ethical decisions.

**5) Four principles:**
- **Autonomy:** the right to make one's own decisions.
- **Justice:** fairness; treating people equally and fairly.
- **Beneficence:** doing good; acting in the client's best interest.
- **Non-maleficence:** doing no harm.

**6) Ethical dilemma:**
A situation where there are conflicting values or principles and no clearly "right" answer. How to deal: identify the problem, gather facts, consider client rights, laws and policies, consult supervisor/team, consider options, act, and document and reflect.

**7) Scenario (wife says "force the food into him"):**
- **What should I do?** Never force-feed. Stay calm, explain respectfully that I can't do that, encourage him (offer preferences, small portions, good positioning and a pleasant environment), document intake and report the weight loss/refusal to the nurse/supervisor, and ask the team (dietitian, nurse) to address concerns with the family.
- **Does the client have the right to refuse?** Yes, if capable. Competent clients have the right to refuse food. Refusal should be reported and the reasons explored (pain, swallowing, dentures, depression, preferences).
- **Does the wife have the right to demand force-feeding?** No. Family can voice concerns but cannot demand something that violates the client's rights or is unsafe (battery/abuse). If the client lacks capacity, a legal substitute decision-maker is involved through the health care team, but forced feeding is still not acceptable.
- **Agency policies:** The employer's written rules and procedures guiding staff practice (e.g. feeding, refusal of care, reporting, client rights). Follow them and use them to decide what to do.

## 11. Teamwork and Leadership

**1) Team:**
A group of people with different skills working together toward a common goal.

**2) Goal of a healthcare team:**
To provide safe, effective, quality, client-centred care that meets the client's needs.

**3) Benefits:**
Shared workload, diverse skills and ideas, better client care, support and learning, improved safety, higher job satisfaction.

**4) Challenges:**
Communication problems, conflict, personality differences, unclear roles, unequal workload, gossip, lack of trust, scheduling.

**10) Why understand group dynamics?**
It helps me understand how people interact, roles, communication and conflict, so I can work better with others, solve problems and improve team performance.

**11) Three conditions for effective team development (class answer may vary):**
Clear shared goals, open communication and trust/respect, and clearly defined roles and responsibilities.

**12) Tuckman's five stages:**
1. **Forming:** polite, getting acquainted, unsure of roles.
2. **Storming:** conflict, disagreements, power struggles.
3. **Norming:** cooperation, rules and trust develop.
4. **Performing:** productive teamwork, goals achieved.
5. **Adjourning:** group ends or disbands; reflection and closure.

**13) Extrovert or introvert? (sample):**
I think I'm an introvert (or extrovert; choose yours). Working with the opposite style, an extrovert may talk and act quickly while an introvert needs time to think. To work well together: respect the differences, give quiet team members time to speak, invite input, and for extroverts, listen, wait and give space.

**14) Sensing vs intuitive style:**
- **Sensing:** focuses on facts, details, practical and present information.
- **Intuitive:** focuses on big picture, patterns, possibilities and future ideas.

**15) Team with all similar members:**
It wouldn't work as well. They'd share the same strengths and the same blind spots, there would be gaps in skills, less creativity and problem solving, and role overlap or competition. Different roles and styles balance each other.

**16) Emotional intelligence:**
The ability to recognize, understand and manage my own emotions and to recognize and respond to others' emotions (self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills).

**17) A great leader (sample):**
They listened, were fair and respectful, communicated clearly, led by example, gave feedback and recognition, supported the team in busy times, and admitted mistakes.

**18) How an HCA can be a leader:**
Be reliable and positive, communicate clearly, help coworkers, take initiative, follow policies and model professionalism, respect everyone, accept feedback, resolve conflicts calmly, advocate for clients, and keep learning.
